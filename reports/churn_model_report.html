Executive Summary

Key Drivers of Customer Churn

is_month_to_month
Customers on month-to-month contracts exhibit substantially higher churn risk due to lower contractual commitment compared to customers on long-term contracts.
tenure
Customers with shorter tenure are significantly more likely to churn. This indicates that churn risk is highest during the early stages of the customer lifecycle, highlighting the importance of onboarding and early engagement strategies.
InternetService_Fiber optic
Fiber optic customers demonstrate higher churn rates, potentially driven by higher service costs or elevated expectations regarding service quality.
MonthlyCharges
Higher monthly charges are associated with increased churn risk, suggesting price sensitivity among customers and potential dissatisfaction with perceived value.
PaymentMethod_Electronic check
Customers using electronic check payment methods exhibit higher churn rates, which may reflect payment friction or lower long-term commitment.
InternetService_No
Customers without internet service tend to churn less frequently, which may reflect lower service complexity and fewer unmet expectations.
OnlineSecurity_Yes
Subscription to online security services is associated with lower churn risk, suggesting that customers using protective services are more engaged and reliant on the provider.
PaperlessBilling
Paperless billing is associated with higher churn rates, potentially reflecting a more price-sensitive or digitally transient customer segment.
TechSupport_Yes
Customers with technical support subscriptions are less likely to churn, emphasizing the role of customer support in retention and satisfaction.
num_active_services
Customers subscribed to a higher number of additional services tend to churn less, indicating greater product dependency and higher switching costs.

Conclusion

The selected model demonstrates strong predictive performance and is suitable for identifying customers at high risk of churn. The results can support proactive retention strategies and targeted interventions.
